03 The honesty question

You said you didn't know how to answer this one. Here it is without the jargon.

You ran two gradients. Each peak came off the column once in each run — at one particular %B. Unknown-1 eluted near 34 %B in the 15-minute run and near 31 %B in the 45-minute run. Those two points are the only places the model has ever been checked for that compound.

The model draws a straight line through them. That is what LSS is. Then it will happily evaluate that line anywhere you ask.

Three things to decide

a · How far is too far?

The research proposes gentle out to about 0.6 window-widths, strong beyond — 0.6 preserves today's "2× outside" behaviour at β = 3. The evidence is your own: run 4 sits 0.26 window-widths outside and scored 0.26% average error, better than in-window run 3's 0.35%. No published number exists for where curvature finally bites; the one candidate figure was behind a paywall and correctly went unused. This is the open half of ticket #44.

b · Per peak, or per method? decided · per peak

The research settled it harder than either of us had: your three peaks are calibrated over 42.7–52.4, 52.9–62.8 and 80.7–90.2 %B. They do not overlap, so a method-level union is calibrated for no peak at all. It becomes a per-peak badge, alongside the early-eluter and prediction-crossing ones.

c · Warn, or refuse? decided · warn

Warnings over blocks, per CLAUDE.md. This also settles something concrete: session.py::_check_candidate currently raises when the candidate's %B range differs from scouting. It becomes a warning path, and SPEC §4's wording changes with it.

04 The Cockpit question

Open the app and look at the left side. The split you see there is about to break.

The sidebar holds %B start = 5 and %B end = 95. The left rail holds the Candidate block: a tG slider and an initial hold slider. That split says something specific — the %B range is a fact about the experiment you ran, and tG is the knob you turn. Both scouting runs and the candidate share the range, which is why it sits up in the constants.

Gradient freedom makes %B two different things at once:

  • What you actually ran — 5→95, fixed, historical, the thing the fit was built from.
  • What you're asking about — could be 20→60, and now it's a knob.

They cannot stay one widget. Does the Candidate block grow its own %B start and end? Does the sidebar get relabelled "as acquired"? Does the screen show both side by side, so you can see how far you have wandered — which would also be the natural home for the warning from section 03?

Multi-segment makes it harder still, because a candidate stops being three numbers and becomes a list: hold 2 min at 20 %B, ramp to 60 over 15 min, ramp to 95 over 5. That is a small table, not a pair of sliders. Where does it live, and does a %B-versus-time plot appear beside the chromatogram so you can see the program you have built?

You don't design this — you react to it. I build three versions inside the real Cockpit, you flip between them and say "that one, but with the table from the other."

05 Tomorrow at the bench

The research landed after the first run list was written, and replaced it. Here is why, and what to run instead.

Where a peak elutes depends on the candidate gradient only through its steepness, s* = t0·Δφ/tG — Guillarme 2022 Eq. 8 and den Uijl 2021 Eq. 10, arrived at independently. Two gradients with different %B ranges but the same s* put every peak at the same composition.

So a different %B range does not, by itself, test anything the gradient time does not already test. It moves along the same axis under another name. The first three runs I proposed all landed inside the existing bracket — three more interpolations.

The replacements below are chosen on s*, computed from your fitted parameters. Note runs A and 3 in the table: their traces are parallel. That is the point of run A.
