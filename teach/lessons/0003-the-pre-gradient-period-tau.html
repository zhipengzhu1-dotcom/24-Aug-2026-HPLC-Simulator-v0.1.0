The pre-gradient period τ

Lesson 2 told you to ignore one term in the retention equation for now. Here it is: τ, and why it's load-bearing enough that your own spec makes it a required input, not a default.

What τ actually is

The retention equation from Lesson 2, in full:

$$t_R = \underbrace{\tau}_{\text{new today}} + t_0 + \frac{t_0}{b_e}\ln\!\Big[b_e\big(k_0-\tfrac{\tau}{t_0}\big)+1\Big]$$

$\tau = t_D + t_\text{init}$ — two things you already deal with at the bench, just not usually as one combined number:

  • $t_D$, dwell time — the gradient your pump mixes at $t=0$ doesn't reach the column instantly. It has to sweep through the mixer and tubing between the pump and the column inlet — the dwell volume $V_D$. Until it arrives, the column still sees the starting composition $\varphi_0$.
  • $t_\text{init}$, initial hold — any isocratic hold you programmed on purpose before the ramp starts (your method has 0.5 min of it).

Both have the same effect on a band sitting in the column: nothing changes for those $\tau$ minutes, no matter what the method's programmed clock says is happening. The column's clock and the pump's clock are offset by $\tau$.

Sit with that comparison: dwell time alone (0.9375 min) is already longer than your column's entire dead time (0.6 min). Before the gradient front has even reached the column inlet, more time has passed than it takes an unretained peak to sweep straight through the column. $\tau$ isn't a rounding footnote here — it's bigger than $t_0$ itself.

The evidence this is worth requiring, not defaulting

This isn't a theoretical worry — it's a finding from your own validation research (ticket #4, sourced from docs/research/validation-datasets.md):

From the den Uijl reference datasets Set X: dropping just the 0.25 min programmed hold degraded median |ΔtR| from 0.11% to a systematic +1.09% bias.
Set Y: using a geometry-estimated $t_0$ instead of a measured marker left a +0.41% bias.

Your own validation/PROTOCOL.md — the bench protocol you wrote for characterizing this exact instrument — states the general form of the same finding directly:1 "Sloppy dwell/t0 values — not the engine — are the usual reason such bars fail (+1% systematic bias per dropped term)." This is exactly why SPEC.md §4 makes dwell "required, no silent default" and $t_0$ "measured-first" with a labeled, confidence-lowering fallback — not caution for its own sake, but a direct response to a measured failure mode.

1. validation/PROTOCOL.md also documents how you actually measure $V_D$ properly instead of trusting the spec sheet: replace the column with a zero-dead-volume union, run water→water+0.1% acetone, and take $t_D$ as the time to the midpoint of the absorbance rise. Your current 0.375 mL is spec-sheet, not measured — the cheapest remaining accuracy upgrade on this method.
