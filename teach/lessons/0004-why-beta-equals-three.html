Why β = 3

Lesson 2 established that two runs sample two points on each peak's $(\varphi, \ln k)$ line. This lesson is about how far apart those two points need to be — and why your own scouting runs (tG = 15 & 45) already got it right.

The question Lesson 2 left open

$\beta = t_{G,2}/t_{G,1}$ is just the ratio of your two scouting gradient times. Nothing in the algebra of Lesson 2 says it has to be any particular number — any two different $t_G$ values give two equations for the two unknowns $(k_0, S_e)$. So why does the spec (and the literature) insist on about 3, not "any two values you like"?

Two points too close together

Picture fitting a line's slope from two points that are almost on top of each other, versus two points spread well apart. In the first case, a tiny error in either point's height swings the fitted slope wildly; in the second, the same error barely moves it. That's literally what's happening here — the two points are each peak's elution composition, $\varphi_{e,1}$ and $\varphi_{e,2}$, and the "slope" is $S_e$.

The same 0.6-second wobble — the kind of run-to-run noise you'd get from ordinary injection and integration variability, nothing exotic — costs 0.13% at β=3 but 8.80% at β=1.05. That's roughly a 70× difference in how much a fixed amount of measurement noise gets amplified into parameter error, purely from how close together the two runs were.

So why not push β even higher?

If separation only helps, why not run tG and 10·tG? Because LSS is an approximation — $\ln k$ vs. $\varphi$ is only close to a straight line, over a bounded range. The wider you stretch the two scouting runs apart, the more of that real, gentle curvature gets folded into your fitted "straight line," rather than averaged away as noise. This is exactly the phenomenon behind the sign-flip you saw in Lesson 1's headline result — the +0.35% bias at tG=25 flipping to −0.26% at tG=60 was diagnosed as mild LSS curvature, not a dwell error. Push β too far and you're intentionally inviting more of that.

So the real design problem is a trade-off: too close (β near 1) and ordinary timing noise dominates the fit; too far apart and real curvature starts contaminating what's supposed to be a straight-line fit. β ≈ 3 is where three independent sources land as the sweet spot:

  • Molnár (2002), describing DryLab's own worked example: two gradients "in 40 and 120 min" — β = 3 exactly.
  • Molnár, quoting Snyder's guidance on how far any one variable can be changed while still treated independently: "a factor of three in gradient elution time."
  • Guillarme et al. (2022): "the usual ratio recommended for accurate retention modeling is 3 only."

den Uijl et al. (2021) add the honest caveat worth keeping: β is "often assumed to be at least three," but what matters more is how close your scouting runs bracket the gradients you actually intend to predict. β=3 is a good default, not a law handed down from above.

Your own runs already did this tG = 15 and tG = 45 → β = 45/15 = 3.0, exactly. Not a coincidence — the protocol you built for this instrument (validation/PROTOCOL.md) specified it going in, on the strength of the same three sources above.

How the spec turns this into a guardrail

SPEC.md §4 doesn't hard-block on β — it recommends tG and ~3·tG, then shows a warning below a spacing ratio of ~2.5 and a strong warning below ~1.2, never a hard refusal. That matches den Uijl's caveat directly: a tighter spacing isn't invalid, it's just worse-conditioned, and sometimes matching your actual prediction targets should win over chasing the textbook ratio.
