Lesson 1 · Orientation

Three things called "the prototype"

Before any of the math — a map of what actually exists on disk right now, because the word "prototype" has been doing triple duty and that's worth untangling first.

Over the last few sessions, three different things got called "the prototype." They prove different things, they live in different places, and only one of them produced the number you actually care about. Here they are, in the order they were built:

1 · A UI mock (throwaway)

Three Streamlit layout variants, on branch prototype/main-screen, file prototype_main_screen.py. The math inside is cartoon math — clearly labeled as fake, there only to see how a chromatogram, a peak table, and a fit-results panel sit together on screen. The Cockpit variant won: sidebar for method constants, chromatogram as the hero element, peak table and fit results side by side. That layout decision is real and carries forward into the spec (§7) — the numbers behind it never were.

2 · A live engine preview (this is "the simulation")

About 50 lines of Python, written and run in conversation only — it was never committed anywhere in the repo. This is the one that matters: it implemented the real two-run LSS fit and retention-time prediction from the sourced equations, and ran it against your own lab data. When you've been calling something "the simulation," this is it.

3 · The planned product (not built yet)

No production code exists. What exists is a specification — SPEC.md at the repo root, currently uncommitted, awaiting your review (ticket #11 on the wayfinder map). It describes a thin Streamlit app sitting over a pure hplcsim engine library. Building it is deliberately out of scope for this session — see the last section below.

The number that matters belongs to #2 From two scouting runs (tG = 15 & 45 min, three unknown peaks), the engine preview blindly predicted your confirmation runs:
  • tG = 25 (interpolation): 13.861 / 16.729 / 24.383 predicted vs. 13.787 / 16.658 / 24.358 measured → 0.35% average error, 0.53% worst
  • tG = 60 (extrapolation, 1.33× outside the bracket): 25.480 / 32.236 / 50.774 predicted vs. 25.587 / 32.320 / 50.821 measured → 0.26% average error

That's the headline result the rest of this workspace exists to explain. Lesson 2 starts on the model that produced it — the LSS equation and why two runs are enough to fit it.
